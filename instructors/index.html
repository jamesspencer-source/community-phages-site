Teach in Community Phages.

Community Phages depends on instructors who like teaching at the bench. Faculty, graduate students, postdocs, staff, alumni, and partner instructors help RCC students run experiments, interpret mixed results, and decide what to do next.

Resources Get in touch

What instructors do.

Instructors keep students safe at the bench, explain protocols, help troubleshoot experiments, and help students turn uncertain results into defensible conclusions.

Instructor

Bench teaching

Instructors show students how to set up plates, handle bacterial cultures, read lawns and plaques, repeat experiments, and keep useful notebooks.

Instructor

Research troubleshooting

Instructors help students decide what a plate, gel, titer, image, or genome result means and what experiment should come next.

Instructor

Poster mentoring

Students need help choosing the right evidence, writing claims they can defend, and practicing a clear explanation of their isolate.

Instructor work by week.

Early weeks are heavy on bench orientation. Mid-summer is reading plates, repeating experiments, microscopy, DNA work, and bioinformatics. The end of the program is figures, interpretation, practice talks, and poster presentations.

1

Teach the method

Walk students through pipetting, sterile technique, plate setup, sample handling, and notebook expectations.

2

Troubleshoot the result

Help students read lawns and plaques, decide what to repeat, and make sense of results that are ambiguous or incomplete.

3

Put data in context

Bring in phage structure, host range, bacterial defenses, genome annotation, and comparisons to known phages.

4

Support the poster

Help students choose figures, write claims they can defend, and practice explaining their isolate to an audience.
