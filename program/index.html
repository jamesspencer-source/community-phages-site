The 2026 program runs May 18-July 9, Monday through Thursday from 9:00 AM-2:00 PM. Students start with safety and pipetting, then move into environmental sampling, plaques, purification, titers, microscopy, DNA, genome comparisons, and final posters.

A typical lab day.

Students are usually on campus Monday-Thursday from 9:00 AM-2:00 PM. Most days include setup, bench work or bioinformatics, notebook time, data review, and a decision about the next experiment.

Morning

Set up the experiment

Review the protocol, what the result might show, and how the experiment relates to host bacteria or phage biology.

Midday

Run the work

Plate samples, pick plaques, prepare lysates, run gels, image phages, compare strains, or analyze sequence data.

Close

Record and interpret

Record observations, calculations, gels, plaques, images, and next steps while the work is still fresh.

Final posters.

Students collect samples, purify phages, document measurements, analyze DNA sequence when available, and use those data in final posters.
